# Premise: <working title>

## Logline
One or two sentences: who, what they want, what stands in the way, what's at
stake.

## Dramatic question
The question the player wants answered by the end.

## Theme
The thematic question, phrased as a question about how to live. Then two to
four competing answers the story will put in conflict (these become
characters and the antagonist in the cast stage).

## The world in brief
One paragraph: what kind of world, what is wrong with it, why now.

## The antagonist in brief
Who or what opposes the party, what they want, why the player might
understand them.

## Shape
The macro-structure in a few sentences: how the story opens, the midpoint
turn, the ending's emotional note. How the open world fits (when does the
world open up, and why).

## Tone
Where on the scale from light to grim; what kind of humor; what the story is
never going to do.

## Signature moments
Three to five images or scenes that would be the trailer: specific, not
generic.

## What makes it ours
Which familiar ideas this uses, and how it makes each one specific. Why a
player who knows FF4 and FF6 would still be surprised.

## Risks
What might not work, and how later stages should test it.
