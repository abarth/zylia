# Story Review

How story drafts are critiqued. A review is a separate document written by a
different agent than the author, saved as `<draft>.review.md` next to the
draft. The reviewer's job is to find what isn't compelling yet, not to
approve.

## Review format

```markdown
---
reviews: <path of the draft>
reviewer: <who>
---

## Scores
| Criterion | Score (1-5) | One-line reason |

## Strongest element
What to protect in revisions.

## Biggest problem
The one change that would most improve the draft.

## Other issues
Numbered, each pointing at a specific part of the draft.

## Questions for the author
Things the draft leaves unclear.
```

A draft is ready for the owner when no criterion scores below 3 and the
biggest problem has been addressed. Scores are for comparing drafts and
tracking improvement, not a grade to game.

## Criteria

Each criterion maps to a section of [principles.md](principles.md). Not every
criterion applies to every stage; mark those "n/a".

1. **Hook**: Would a player want to know what happens next after the
   opening? Is the dramatic question clear and urgent?
2. **Theme**: Is there a real thematic question? Do the characters embody
   different answers to it? Does the story argue rather than preach?
3. **Characters**: Does each have a want, need and wound? Do they make
   choices that matter? Are they distinct from each other in voice, look and
   role? Do their arcs press on each other?
4. **Antagonist**: Understandable goal? Active, escalating, winning at least
   once? A mirror of the theme? Frightening or compelling on screen?
5. **Stakes**: Do they rise? Do they stay personal? Do victories cost
   something?
6. **Causality**: "Therefore/but" or "and then"? Any coincidences that
   rescue the heroes?
7. **Setups and payoffs**: Are reveals prepared? Do twists recontextualize?
   Is every setup in the threads ledger?
8. **Play fit**: Do the beats give good places to explore and fight? Do
   abilities and party changes fit the story? Does it survive the player
   doing things in a different order?
9. **Pacing**: Tension and release? A set piece per chapter? Exposition in
   small doses?
10. **Originality and specificity**: Specific details? Tropes used on
    purpose? Nothing borrowed from existing works?
11. **Consistency**: Does it agree with all canon it depends on? Are new
    names in the glossary?

## Tests a reviewer should run

- **The one-sentence test**: can you state the draft's premise, theme and
  each character's arc in one sentence each? If not, it's not clear yet.
- **The swap test**: give a line of dialogue or a decision to a different
  character. If nothing breaks, the characters aren't distinct enough.
- **The removal test**: remove a character or chapter. If nothing breaks,
  it isn't earning its place.
- **The villain's-diary test**: tell the plot from the antagonist's side. Do
  they have a plan, and does it make sense?
- **The "why don't they just" test**: what would a sensible person do
  instead? Does the story account for it?
- **The replay test**: for each twist, list the earlier clues.
