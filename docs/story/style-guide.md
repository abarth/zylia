# Writing Style Guide

**Status: Draft.**

## Tone

The approved premise sets the story's tone. Until then, the default is:
earnest, adventurous, with humor in the everyday and weight in the big
moments, like FF4 and FF6. Characters speak like people, not like fantasy
novels: no "thee" and "thou" unless a character's voice calls for it.

## Dialogue

- **Short lines.** A text box holds about 38 characters per line and 3 lines
  (2 with a speaker name). One idea per line; split long speeches into
  several lines rather than walls of text.
- **Every line earns its place.** It reveals character, advances the plot,
  gives the player information, or is funny.
- **Voices are distinct.** Each character's canon document defines their
  voice; read a line without the speaker name and you should still know who
  said it.
- **Town NPCs** give hints, rumors, lore, or a small story of their own.
  Some should change what they say as the story moves on.
- **No modern slang or real-world references.**

## Naming

- Invented names should be easy to read and say, with a consistent flavor per
  culture (e.g. one region uses hard consonants, another uses long vowels).
  Record each culture's naming rules in the world document.
- Avoid names that are existing Final Fantasy characters or places.
- Spell, item and monster names are our own; we can nod to FF conventions
  (tiers, a Potion/Ether/Phoenix Down-like trio) without copying names in the
  finished game. Current item names are placeholders.
- Every invented name goes in [canon/glossary.md](canon/glossary.md) once
  approved.

## Formatting in content

- Plain text only; no markup yet.
- Use a speaker name for anyone the player can identify; omit it for
  narration, signs and system messages ("Found Potion!").
